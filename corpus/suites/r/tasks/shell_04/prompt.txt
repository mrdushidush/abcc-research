SHELL-04 -- the timeout does not kill the process tree, so a 30 s budget can take minutes.

THE FILE, and the only one you may change: `crates/claudette/src/test_runner.rs`. It is 180 lines,
and it already has a `#[cfg(test)] mod tests` at the bottom to put your test in.

THE DEFECT, measured on this repo. `child.kill()` is `TerminateProcess`/`SIGKILL` on the direct
child ALONE. When that child is a launcher -- `powershell`, `cmd`, `npm`, `cargo`, one of git's
helpers -- its own children survive and keep holding the stdout/stderr write ends they inherited.
The pipe therefore never reaches EOF, `read_to_string` inside `spawn_pipe_reader` never returns,
and `join_reader`'s `handle.join()` blocks for the surviving grandchild's ENTIRE lifetime. A 3
second budget was measured returning after 60.1 seconds. This affects every `run_git`, `git_in`,
`run_tests` and `diagnostics` call in the crate.

Two sites: the timeout branch in `run_command_with_timeout` (`let _ = child.kill();
let _ = child.wait();`), and `join_reader`.

THE FIX -- three parts, in this shape:

1. Kill the TREE, not just the child. On Windows run `taskkill /T /F /PID <pid>` through an
   ordinary `std::process::Command`. On Unix give the child its own process group at spawn with
   `std::os::unix::process::CommandExt::process_group(0)` behind `#[cfg(unix)]`, then signal the
   whole group with `kill -KILL -<pid>`. Keep `child.kill()` and `child.wait()` underneath as the
   fallback.

2. Put a DEADLINE on `join_reader`, so a reader that will never see EOF cannot block forever.
   `JoinHandle::join()` has no timeout, so restructure it: have the reader thread drain into an
   `Arc<Mutex<Vec<u8>>>` and signal completion over an `std::sync::mpsc` channel, then
   `recv_timeout` on that channel. A reader abandoned at the deadline then still hands back
   everything it captured, instead of losing the partial output.

3. A TEST, in the `mod tests` that is already there, which FAILS on the code as it stands now.
   Spawn a child that spawns a grandchild which inherits the pipes and outlives it, give the call a
   short budget, and assert it returns well inside a generous margin. Assert a BOUND, never an
   exact duration -- this runs on loaded CI runners. The existing test in that module already shows
   the portable pattern for reaching a subprocess here (`python`, with a skip-if-absent guard);
   reuse it.

HARD CONSTRAINTS -- a change that breaks either of these cannot land:

* NO NEW DEPENDENCIES. Do not touch `Cargo.toml`. The Windows Job Object that the obvious fix
  would reach for is NOT available to you: this crate has no `windows`, `winapi` or `libc`
  dependency. Standard library only.
* NO `unsafe`. The crate sets `unsafe_code = "forbid"`, so raw FFI will not even compile.

THE GATE grades the tree, not your description of it. All three must pass:

* `cargo test` -- every test passes.
* `cargo fmt --check -- --color=never` -- must exit 0. Watch line lengths; one over-long line
  fails this rung.
* `cargo clippy --all-targets -- -D warnings` -- must exit 0. This crate enables
  `clippy::pedantic`, so it is strict, and `--all-targets` lints your new test too.

After you edit and BEFORE you finish, call `diagnostics` WITH NO ARGUMENTS. It runs
`cargo check --all-targets` plus those same fmt and clippy commands, and it is the only way for you
to see the standard rung before the gate does.

Keep the whole change inside that one file.
